Title: I tried a new motivation method
Tags: Workflow


I remember my professor telling me about his Phd thesis being on gamification of education. he made a program where students could earn in-game currency and other in-game benefits by completing real world tasks. The goal was to help motivate students by combining their video games with their homework.

I recently participated in a very similar gamification technique, using a well-maintained open source software. Before I get to it I need to introduce a few things.

------------------------------

There is a video game modification, called Archipelago. It's called a "multi-world randomizer". "Randomizer" means item locations are randomly shuffled within a game. The "Multi-world" part refers to what the mod does. It links multiple games together in a randomizer, allowing items from one game to be found in another game.

This ends up creating an experience where players can play their favorite single player games with the mod and collaboratively complete their games. Player 1 picks up an item for player 2, and player 2 gets an item for player 1, allowing both of them to progress. Any number of players can be involved, allowing groups of even 10 or more to participate together.

Interestingly, this creates a sense of peer pressure as well. If player 1 does not have their essential item, and they have been waiting a long time for someone to pick it up, they can use a "hint" to see the location of the item they want. Suppose it is in player 7's game. They can go ask player 7 to grab the item so that player 1 can continue playing the game they enjoy.
It also creates a sense of feeling helpful to others. When someone picks up an important item for someone else, often I will see a message in the chat like "Hey John I got your hookshot!" or similar

So far I have played Archipelago at least 5 times. It is quite fun, and the groups I play with have continued to return to play it. The games can take a long time. Even a few weeks if people are playing slow games like Stardew Valley or Dark Souls. 

------------------------------

This brings me to my most recent experience. I wanted to participate in a 2-month long Archipelago, but I did not have the time to play a game on top of doing schoolwork and a job. I asked around online and found out about a program called Taskipelago, which is a task planner designed to be compatible with Archipelago. Essentially, Taskipelago spoofs in-game item locations by using "tasks". Completing a task acts the same as picking up an item.

I configured it to have tasks related to my Thesis, job search, etc. So far I have found the peer pressure to be helpful, and it feels good to get an item for someone else. It's pretty motivating and can even help foster a sense of community even when your work is solo.


Overall, I think it is a good tool for gamification, that can easily be extended to fit large groups. While also accommodating unique player preferences and playstyles.
